Learn Sketchnoting with Phoebe

Somebody talks for nine minutes and says twelve things that matter. Typed as fast as fingers go, the notes come to 286 words, 84 percent of them the speaker's exact words, and two of the twelve never arrive because the typist was still on the last sentence. Captured as a keyword and one line in the listener's own words: 110 words, all twelve, none of them the speaker's. Six sessions on that second habit. What sketchnoting is when you take the drawing anxiety out of it, the five shapes and the seven layouts, the hierarchy that makes a page readable by a colleague, and a bench that plays the talk at speaking pace and measures what you kept.

The six sessions 🎯 · one talk, six captures

The running situation is Daybreak's autumn roast talk: the founder of the coffee-subscription company from the SQL, dataviz, visual-storytelling and mind-mapping courses gives the roastery team nine minutes on where the launch stands. Twelve things are worth keeping. Every session captures the same nine minutes a different way, so the only thing that ever changes is what you kept and where it sits on the page.

✏️ Session 1 · easy

Ideas, not art

Listening with a pen. The four moves, the drawing objection answered with Rohde's own scale, and three separate literatures read past their headlines: doodling, the drawing effect, pen versus keyboard.

▶ Start here 🔺 Session 2 · easy

Five shapes, one vocabulary

Circle, square, triangle, line, dot. Every icon is a recipe of two or three, and the twenty icons for your own work are built before the meeting so the mark is already in the hand.

twenty icons 📏 Session 3 · bench night

The selection bench

The talk plays one word at a time at 150 words per minute. You capture. The bench counts ideas kept of twelve, verbatim overlap by the research's own definition, and what each idea cost. Four presets and one trap.

★ the bench 🗂️ Session 4 · medium

Seven layouts, one page

Rohde's seven patterns drawn small, and how to pick one from the agenda before the speaker starts. The same twelve ideas on a grid and on a path, and which page a colleague can find something in.

skeleton first 🔍 Session 5 · medium

Readable by someone else

Three weights and one accent. Hierarchy, one rung of the lettering ladder, containers and connectors, and the thirty-second scan: hand the page to a colleague and count.

thirty seconds 📨 Session 6 · hands-on

Live, then shared

One real twenty-minute talk captured live with everything so far, then the one-page recap that leaves your desk. Final scorecard on a page somebody else will actually read.

What is honest here 🧾

The bench in session 3 computes its numbers from what you typed against the transcript. The one-line verdict is a rule of thumb and is labelled as one on the widget. There is no recall model, because the evidence does not support one, and there is no drawing score, because a browser cannot see a pen.

Ideas captured, words, lines, words per idea, own wordsReally counted in the capture box against the twelve keyed ideas; the chips show which registered
