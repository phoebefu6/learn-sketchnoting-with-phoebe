limits of the bench. by Phoebe Fu

Research date: 2026-09-18. Fetch method mattered: every number below came from text
extracted with `curl` plus PyMuPDF from the PDF named, or from the raw HTML of the page
named, never from a summariser. A fetch summariser produced a confident, fully invented

## The course in one line

Sketchnoting is listening with a pen. The drawing is the part people fear and the part
that matters least; the part that matters is choosing what survives, and that part has
evidence behind it. Six sessions on the five shapes, the seven layouts, the hierarchy that
makes a page readable by somebody else, and a bench that measures the one thing a browser
can measure: what you kept.

**Running situation:** Daybreak, the coffee subscription company from the SQL, dataviz,
visual-storytelling and mind-mapping courses. The founder gives the roastery team a
nine-minute talk about the autumn roast. About 560 words, twelve things worth keeping.
Every session captures that talk a different way.

### The evidence, kept as three literatures

Every sketchnoting book conflates three bodies of research. They have different effect
sizes, different reliability, and exactly one of them has been replicated at scale.
**None of them tested sketchnoting as taught.**

**Doodling and attention - Andrade 2009/2010.** *Applied Cognitive Psychology* 24(1) 100-106.
Tier A, full PDF text.

| Fact | Numbers |
|---|---|
| N = 40, adults 18-55, between-subjects; a 2.5-minute monotonous mock phone message, eight party-goers' names to monitor | - |
| The "doodling" was **shading printed shapes**, not free doodling ("in case they felt self-conscious"); mean 36.3 shapes shaded | - |
| Monitoring: doodling **7.7** (SD 0.6) vs control **6.9** (SD 1.3); Mann-Whitney U = 124, p = .01 one-tailed | - |
| Surprise recall: doodling **7.5** vs control **5.8**, **29% more**; F(1,38) = 6.0, p = .02 | - |
| The paper's own caveat: with monitoring as a covariate the effect drops to **marginal, F(1,37) = 3.8, p = .058** | - |
| The author says it is not known "whether the benefits of the shading task extend to naturalistic doodling" | - |

Licenses: "a light concurrent task during a boring stream protected attention in one
40-person lab study." Does not license: "doodling improves comprehension" or any number
about meetings.

**The drawing effect - Wammes, Meade & Fernandes 2016 and after.** *QJEP* 69(9) 1752-76.
Tier A (PubMed abstract); the full text was unfetchable, so **no per-condition recall
proportions and no "twice as many" ratio are printed anywhere**.

| Fact | Tier |
|---|---|
| Seven free-recall experiments; drawn single concrete words were recalled better than written ones; ruled out depth of processing, imagery and picture superiority as sufficient; held in pure-list designs (Exp 7) | A |
| Fernandes, Wammes & Meade 2018, *Current Directions* 27(5): the integrated-components model (write < elaborate or picture < trace < draw); robust to a 2-day delay; smaller or absent under speeded recognition; 13 dementia patients remembered "almost exclusively" the drawn words | A (full PDF) |
| Mykoniatis, Smith & Drake 2024, *Empirical Studies of the Arts* 43(2): n = 91, scientific terms, pure lists: "**no difference across conditions**"; drawing ability, working memory and familiarity predicted recall | A (OpenAlex abstract) |
| Namias & Huff 2025, *Memory* 33(10): the effect held in both designs but was "more robust when drawings were generated than replicated", and larger in mixed lists partly because of a **writing cost** in mixed lists | A (OpenAlex + PubMed) |

Read together: real, design-sensitive, and inflated by the design in which drawn and written
items share a list. "Draw everything" is not the experiment; "draw the important bits" is
closer, and it has not been run.

**Handwriting versus typing - the famous one, and its collapse.**

| Study | What it found | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Mueller & Oppenheimer 2014, *Psychological Science* 25(6): Study 1, 65 students, five TED talks | Factual recall **no difference** (F(1,55) = 0.014, p = .91). Conceptual: laptop M = -0.156 vs longhand M = 0.154, F(1,55) = 9.99, p = .03. Laptop notes had more words (309.6 vs longhand), **d = 1.4**; verbatim overlap **14.6% vs 8.8%**, d = 0.94; more overlap predicted worse performance (beta = -0.43, p = .005). Study 2: telling laptop users not to transcribe "was completely ineffective" (p = .97) | A (full PDF) |
| The 2018 corrigendum (*Psych Science* 29(9) 1565-68): with lecture modelled as random, Study 1 conceptual becomes F(1, 4.09) = 8.05, **p = .046**, about four denominator degrees of freedom | A (via Urry's footnote) |
| Morehead, Dunlosky & Rawson 2019, *Educational Psychology Review* 31(3): added eWriter and no-notes groups; "performance did not consistently differ between any groups... including a group who did not take notes"; meta-analysis of direct replications "small (nonsignificant) effects favoring longhand" | A (ERIC abstract) |
| Urry et al. 2021, *Psychological Science*, preregistered direct replication, laptop n = 74, longhand n = 68 | Factual .63 vs .62, g = -0.08 [-0.41, 0.25], equivalent to zero. Conceptual .74 vs .70, **g = -0.13**, the direction flipped. **The mechanism replicated**: word count g = -0.90, verbatim overlap **12.97% vs 8.13%**, g = -0.85. Mini meta-analysis over eight lab studies: quiz effect "near zero irrespective of item type" | A (full PDF) |
| Allen et al. 2020, *Southern Communication Journal* 85(3): 14 studies, 3,075 participants, electronic note-taking reduced outcomes, average **r = -.142** | A (OpenAlex abstract) |

**How the pages state it.** Typing makes you transcribe (large, replicated every time).
Transcription correlates with worse conceptual performance (replicated). The jump to
"writing by hand makes you learn better" did not survive two replications and an
eight-study aggregation, while a 14-study classroom meta-analysis still finds a small effect
the other way. Sketchnoting's real case is the **behaviour**, fewer words and forced
selection, not the pen. That is why the bench measures selection.

**Sketchnoting itself - thin, small, mostly not about recall.**

| Study | Finding | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Ohmes, Schlesier & Moschner 2024, *ZfE*: 99 fifth-graders, five German lessons; **negative emotions decreased** in the sketchnote group, not in controls; **no achievement or recall outcome claimed** | A (full PDF) |
| Tidy, Burnham & Elkington 2022, *Science & Justice*: forensic students sketchnoted after each lecture hour, shared on a board with feedback; students called it "Helpful", "Refreshing", "Creative"; "a marked increase in the lower grade boundaries", **no control, no effect size** | A (abstract) |
| 2024-2026 titles exist (marketing education, nursing, e-textiles, a CHI paper on revitalising legacy sketchnotes with AI) - **metadata only, none read** | C | not quoted |

**The single most honest sentence available, and the pages say it:** no fetched study tests
sketchnoting as taught against ordinary notes on a work-relevant outcome with a control
group and an effect size. What exists is mechanism evidence: selection beats transcription;
drawing is an encoding act; a light task protects attention.

## The bench (`assets/sn-live.js`)

The founder's talk (557 words as tokenised) plays one word at a time at 130, 150 or 175
words per minute. You capture in a box while it plays. Every number is computed from the
box against the transcript.

### What is measured

| Metric | How |
|---|---|
| Ideas captured (of 12) | Twelve keyed ideas, each with a small cue list; an idea counts if any cue appears in the capture. The twelve light up as chips |
| Verbatim overlap | Mueller and Oppenheimer's definition: share of capture words that sit inside a run of three or more consecutive words also consecutive in the transcript |
| Longest verbatim run | Longest such stretch, in words |
| Words captured, lines | Whitespace tokens; non-empty lines |
| Words per idea | Captured words over ideas captured |
| Your own words | Share of capture words that appear nowhere in the transcript (numbers excluded) |
| Capture time | Seconds from play to stop, if the player was used |

### What is a heuristic

The one-line verdict, labelled "heuristic" on the widget. Rules, in order: nothing
captured; under six ideas; overlap above 40%; ten or more ideas at under four words each
(too thin); ten or more ideas, overlap at or under 25%, at most fourteen words per idea
(selected, own words); otherwise complete or most-of-it.

**What it does not measure: drawing.** Icons, lettering and layout are invisible to it.
That is stated on the widget and in session 3. The anti-preset stands for the time those
things cost.

### The four presets

| id | Label | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `transcribe` | Type everything | A hurried typist: small words dropped, runs out before the end |
| `keywords` | Keywords only | One or two words per idea, nothing else |
| `keyline` | Keyword and one line, my words | A keyword to find it by, then the idea in the learner's words |
| `pretty` | **ANTI** Draw everything beautifully | Five lines; every idea got an icon and a banner; the talk kept going |

### Verified ladder

Derived headlessly (Playwright, 1100px viewport) on 2026-09-19 via `window.SN_LIVE`,
before any page quoted it.

| Preset | Ideas | Verbatim | Longest run | Words | Words/idea | Own words | Verdict (heuristic) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Type everything | **10** / 12 | **84%** | 44 | 286 | 28.6 | 2% | A transcript, not notes |
| Keywords only | 12 / 12 | 0% | 0 | 36 | 3 | 14% | Complete, and too thin to read next week |
| Keyword and one line, my words | **12** / 12 | **0%** | 0 | **110** | 9.2 | 35% | Selected, in your own words |
| **ANTI** Draw everything beautifully | **4** / 12 | 0% | 0 | 24 | 6 | 63% | Less than half the talk survived |

Three rows carry the session's hardest lessons:

- **Typing everything loses ideas.** 286 words, 84% of them the speaker's, and two of the
  twelve never made it, because the typist was still on the pricing paragraph when the
  photos and the checklist went past. 110 chosen words kept all twelve.
- **Keywords alone are complete and useless.** 12 of 12 at three words each is a page you
  cannot read next week. The rung above it costs 74 more words.
- **The anti-preset is the honest cost of drawing.** Four ideas survived. The bench cannot
  see that the icons were lovely; nobody reading the page next week can either.

The player was checked at 150 wpm: ten words appeared in four seconds and the capture timer
read 4s.

### Honest limits

- **Selection, not drawing.** Stated on the widget. A browser cannot grade a sketch.
- **Cue matching is exact on stems.** "Sidamo" counts; a private abbreviation nobody else
  would recognise might not. The chips show which ideas registered, so a miss is visible.
- **Verbatim overlap is a three-gram rule**, the one the research used. Reordered phrases
  score as own words even when the vocabulary is borrowed.
- **The verdict is a rule of thumb** and says so.

## Not covered, by design

- Rohde's drawing exercises and worksheets, the Workbook's advanced lettering: the paid
  core of two books. The five elements are taught; the exercises are his.
- Neill's 128 activities, icon pack and one-page process summary templates. His module
  shape is credited as a structural influence.
- The Handbook's fifteen guest sketchnoter spreads, Brown's figure art, Gray's alphabet
  artwork. Every drawing on these pages was made for them.
- Graphic recording at wall scale, Gamestorming facilitation. Named in session 6 as a
  different profession.
- Digital tooling (iPad, Procreate, GoodNotes). One line in session 6.
- Colour theory. One accent pen, used for hierarchy only, in session 5.
- Paivio's dual coding as a theory; Mayer; levels of processing. The results are cited; the
  theories are not taught, and Paivio's primary sources were not fetched.
